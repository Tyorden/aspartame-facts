# Human Cancer Epidemiology: What the Big Cohorts Actually Found

*Scope note: this brief covers the human observational cancer literature — cohort design, sample sizes, effect sizes, confounding. It deliberately does **not** re-argue the July 2023 IARC/JECFA split (see `jecfa-iarc-2023.md`) and treats the three IARC-cited liver-cancer cohorts only where this brief adds numbers or post-2023 replication that brief does not carry.*

## Bottom line

There is no single "aspartame cohort study." There are roughly a dozen large prospective cohorts, they disagree, and the disagreement tracks study design more than it tracks biology. The two cohorts that measured **aspartame itself** at the largest scale and longest follow-up — NIH-AARP (473,984 people) and the Nurses' Health Studies (~30 years, 10,814 breast cancers) — found nothing. The two cohorts reporting positive associations — NutriNet-Santé 2022 and the CPS-II Nutrition Cohort 2026 — report hazard ratios of 1.13–1.22, and **both lack a coherent dose-response**, which the authors of each say themselves. Meanwhile the strongest single explanatory variable in this entire literature is not aspartame: it is that people who are already heavier, already diabetic, or already dieting are the people who drink diet soda. A defensible summary is: *no cohort has produced a signal that survives dose-response and replication, but the positive studies are real studies by serious groups and should not be waved away.*

## Key findings

**1. NIH-AARP found nothing, at high measured aspartame doses.** 285,079 men and 188,905 women aged 50–71, baseline 1995–96, >5 years follow-up; 1,888 hematopoietic cancers and 315 malignant gliomas. At ≥600 mg aspartame/day vs none: hematopoietic cancer RR **0.98 (95% CI 0.76–1.27)**. Glioma at ≥400 mg/day: RR **0.73 (0.46–1.15)**, with an *inverse* linear trend (P = 0.05). ([Lim et al., *Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev* 2006;15(9):1654–9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16985027/)) — **strong** for the null on brain/blood cancers; note ≥600 mg/day is roughly 3 cans/day, real exposure not a hypothetical.

**2. NutriNet-Santé is the study most often cited against aspartame — and its exposure levels are tiny.** 102,865 French adults, **78.5% women**, mean age 42.2, median follow-up 7.7 years (2009–2021), 3,358 incident cancers. Exposure came from repeated web-based 24-hour dietary records (mean 5.6 records/person) matched to a sweetener composition database. Higher aspartame consumers averaged **47.42 ± 60.75 mg/day** — about a fifth of one diet soda, and roughly 1/60th of the 40 mg/kg ADI for a 70 kg adult. Total cancer HR **1.15 (1.03–1.28)**; breast cancer HR **1.22 (1.01–1.48)**; obesity-related cancer HR **1.15 (1.01–1.32)**. ([Debras et al., *PLOS Medicine* 2022;19(3):e1003950](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8946744/)) — **moderate** as an association, **weak** as evidence of causation.

**3. The Nurses' Health Studies directly contradict NutriNet-Santé's headline breast-cancer result, at far greater power.** ~30 years of follow-up with repeated dietary assessment, **10,814 invasive breast cancers**. Aspartame per 200 mg/day (≈ one 12-oz diet soda): HR **1.00 (0.98–1.03)**. Authors: "In these cohorts, aspartame consumption did not increase breast cancer risk." ([*JNCI* 2025;117(4):795–800; online Oct 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39418201/)) — **strong**. This is the single most useful null in the area, because it is the same endpoint, a bigger case count, and a longer exposure window than the study it refutes.

**4. CPS-II's mortality analysis is essentially null for aspartame-type beverages.** 934,777 cancer-free adults enrolled 1982, followed to 2016 (34 years), 135,093 cancer deaths. Artificially sweetened beverages: associations with obesity-related cancer mortality **disappeared after BMI adjustment**. One residual signal survived: pancreatic cancer mortality HR **1.11 (1.02–1.20)**. Sugar-sweetened beverages, by contrast, held up for colorectal (HR 1.09) and kidney (HR 1.17) cancer after BMI adjustment. ([McCullough et al., *Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev* 2022;31(10):1907–18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36107009/)) — **moderate**. The BMI-adjustment behaviour is the tell: the ASB signal *is* the obesity signal.

**5. The newest positive study is from the American Cancer Society's own cohort, and it is not trivially dismissible.** CPS-II Nutrition Cohort: 100,004 US adults (98% non-Hispanic White, mean age 69), aspartame estimated from low-calorie carbonated beverages (70–180 mg per 12-oz serving) and tabletop packets (20 mg/packet), assessed 1999 and 2003. Consumers vs non-consumers: all cancers aHR **1.13 (1.08–1.18)**; non-obesity-related **1.10 (1.05–1.16)**; obesity-related **1.17 (1.09–1.26)**; digestive obesity-related **1.19 (1.06–1.33)**; female reproductive **1.13 (1.00–1.28)**. The authors flag that **no clear dose-response** was observed. ([*J Nutr*, online 27 Feb 2026](https://jn.nutrition.org/article/S0022-3166(26)00109-4/abstract)) — **moderate**, weakened by the missing dose-response and by an association appearing in *non*-obesity-related cancers too, which is what confounding-by-lifestyle looks like.

**6. The largest meta-analysis of artificially sweetened beverages finds no overall cancer signal.** 14 articles / 17 cohorts, **2,821,922 participants, 34,050 cancer cases**, follow-up 5.1–29.0 years. High vs low ASB intake, overall cancer: pooled RR **1.03 (0.96–1.11)**, I² = 53%, GRADE certainty *very low*. Two site-specific findings: leukaemia RR **1.35 (1.03–1.77)** (I² = 0%, *moderate* certainty, ~15% per 355 mL/day) and colorectal cancer RR **0.78 (0.62–0.99)** (*low* certainty). Funded by the National Natural Science Foundation of China — not industry. ([*Nutrients* 2022;14(21):4445](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9658995/)) — **strong** for the overall null; the leukaemia result is the most interesting unresolved signal in this brief.

**7. Post-IARC replication has gone against the liver-cancer hypothesis.** The Women's Health Initiative (98,786 postmenopausal women, median 20.9 years, 207 liver cancers, 148 chronic liver disease deaths) found ≥1 serving/day vs ≤3/month: **SSB** liver cancer HR **1.85 (1.16–2.96)**; **ASB** liver cancer HR **1.17 (0.70–1.94)** — null — and ASB chronic liver disease mortality HR **0.95 (0.49–1.84)**. Published in *JAMA* on 8 August 2023, weeks after the IARC classification. ([Zhao et al., *JAMA* 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37552302/)) — **strong**. This sits alongside the 2026 11-cohort pooled analysis already covered in `jecfa-iarc-2023.md`.

**8. The IARC-cited pooled US analysis was mostly null.** Jones 2022 pooled NIH-AARP and PLCO: **553,874 participants** — 506,389 without diabetes (839 liver cancers) and 47,485 with diabetes (221 liver cancers). Among people **without** diabetes there was no association for artificially sweetened beverages, sugar-sweetened beverages, or sweetened beverages overall. The positive ASB estimate IARC leaned on came from the **diabetic subgroup only** — a subgroup defined by the exact condition that causes people to switch to diet drinks. ([Jones et al., *Cancer Epidemiol* 2022;79:102201](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35728406/)) — **strong** as a description of the study; the subgroup result is **weak** evidence.

**9. Case-control evidence is null across nine cancer sites.** Italian hospital-based network, 1991–2004: 9,296 cancer cases (oral/pharynx 598, oesophagus 304, colon 1,225, rectum 728, larynx 460, breast 2,569, ovary 1,031, prostate 1,294, kidney 767) vs 7,028 hospital controls. Saccharin ORs 0.46–1.58; aspartame/other sweeteners ORs 0.71–1.62; significant *inverse* trends for breast and ovary. Conclusion: "a lack of association between saccharin, aspartame and other sweeteners and the risk of several common neoplasms." ([Gallus et al., *Ann Oncol* 2007;18(1):40–4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17043096/)) — **moderate** (case-control design, recall bias).

**10. The brain-tumour claim was tested directly and failed.** Olney's 1996 ecological paper argued US brain-tumour incidence rose in two phases with the second (1985–92) coinciding with aspartame ([*J Neuropathol Exp Neurol* 1996;55(11):1115–23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8939194/)). The first phase began in **1975 — six years before aspartame's 1981 approval** — and the design cannot link individual exposure to individual outcome. A case-control study restricted to children born 1981 or later (56 cases, 94 controls) found brain-tumour cases had not consumed more aspartame than controls ([Gurney et al., *JNCI* 1997;89(14):1072–4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9230890/)); the authors themselves noted the small sample and wide CIs. NIH-AARP's 315 gliomas (finding #1) is the definitive test, and it is null.

## Genuine caveats / what cuts the other way

- **The positive studies are not junk.** NutriNet-Santé is a well-conducted cohort with *better* exposure measurement than most (repeated 24-hour records, brand-level sweetener database, not a one-off FFQ). CPS-II NC 2026 is run by the American Cancer Society. Dismissing them because you dislike the result is not a defensible position.
- **Two independent cohorts found ~1.15 for total cancer.** NutriNet-Santé (HR 1.15) and CPS-II NC (aHR 1.13) agree on magnitude for total cancer despite completely different populations, ages, countries and methods. That is not proof, but it is not nothing either.
- **The pancreatic-cancer estimate in CPS-II survived BMI adjustment** (HR 1.11, 1.02–1.20). Most ASB signals vanish when you adjust for weight. This one did not, and remains unexplained.
- **The leukaemia meta-analysis result carries GRADE *moderate* certainty** (RR 1.35, 1.03–1.77, zero heterogeneity, linear dose-response) — the only site-specific finding in this literature with that grade. Schernhammer 2012 also found pooled leukaemia RR 1.42 (1.00–2.02). A site-specific leukaemia question is genuinely open.
- **Most cohorts never measured aspartame.** EPIC, CPS-II 2022, Jones 2022, WHI and most of the meta-analysis inputs measured "artificially sweetened beverages." Since the mid-1990s that category has increasingly meant aspartame *plus* acesulfame-K, sucralose or blends. In NutriNet-Santé, acesulfame-K carried its own total-cancer HR of 1.13 (1.01–1.26), so the two exposures are not separable there either. Attributing an ASB result to aspartame is an inference, not a measurement — in both directions.
- **A confirmed literature error worth knowing.** Stepien 2016 (EPIC) was formally corrected in 2024: the HCC sub-group case count for artificially sweetened soft drinks was misprinted as **n = 101** and is actually **n = 151**. (`jecfa-iarc-2023.md` currently quotes the uncorrected figure — [Correction, *Eur J Nutr* 2024, doi:10.1007/s00394-024-03374-2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00394-024-03374-2).) The HR (1.06, 1.03–1.09 per serving) and the total case counts (191 HCC, 66 intrahepatic bile duct, 236 gallbladder among 477,206 participants over 11.4 years) are unchanged.
- **Reverse causality is not hand-waving; it was quantified.** In NutriNet-Santé, about **one third of higher sweetener consumers started a weight-loss diet within two years**, versus about **one in eight** non-consumers (Prof Kevin McConway's reading of the paper, [Science Media Centre, 24 Mar 2022](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-observational-study-of-sweeteners-and-cancer-risk/)). People switch to sweeteners *because* of their metabolic state, and metabolic state drives cancer risk.
- **A 2023 commentary defending aspartame is industry-authored.** Goodman, Boon & Jack, "Perspectives on recent reviews of aspartame cancer epidemiology," *Global Epidemiology* 2023 — Maia M. Jack is with the **American Beverage Association**, and Julie E. Goodman is a consultant at Gradient. It makes reasonable methodological points but is **industry-linked advocacy** and should never be sole support for a claim.

## Myths in this area and what is actually true

| Claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| "A Harvard study proved diet soda causes lymphoma and leukaemia." | Schernhammer 2012 (NHS + HPFS, 22 years; 1,324 NHL, 285 myelomas, 339 leukaemias) found in **men only** at ≥1 diet soda/day: NHL RR 1.31 (1.01–1.72), multiple myeloma RR 2.02 (1.20–3.40); **nothing in women**. It also found *sugar*-sweetened soda raised NHL risk in men (RR 1.66, 1.10–2.51) — which no aspartame mechanism explains. The authors wrote that the inconsistent sex effects and the sugar-soda finding "do not permit the ruling out of chance." Brigham and Women's Hospital then **withdrew its own press release**: "the consensus of our scientific leaders is that the data is weak, and that BWH Media Relations was premature in the promotion of this work" (24 Oct 2012). Asked whether the study proved aspartame is dangerous, lead author Eva Schernhammer said "No, it does not." ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23097267/); [Embargo Watch, 24 Oct 2012](https://embargowatch.wordpress.com/2012/10/24/harvard-hospital-apologizes-for-promoting-weak-data-on-aspartame-cancer/)) |
| "The French study showed aspartame causes cancer." | It showed an association of HR 1.15 in a self-selected, 78.5%-female, more-educated volunteer cohort, at a mean aspartame intake of 47 mg/day. In absolute terms the estimate corresponds to roughly **4 extra cancer cases per 1,000 people over 8 years** (McConway, Science Media Centre). Dose-response was non-monotonic — for some outcomes the *lower*-consumer group had higher risk than the higher-consumer group despite a tenfold intake difference. |
| "Aspartame causes brain tumours — look at the incidence curve after 1981." | The rise began in 1975. Individual-level tests (NIH-AARP, 315 gliomas; Gurney 1997 in children born after approval) are null. |
| "No study has ever found anything." | False. NutriNet-Santé, CPS-II NC 2026, Schernhammer 2012, the leukaemia meta-analysis estimate, and the EPIC HCC estimate are all positive. They are weak, inconsistent and confounded — but they exist, and a site that claims otherwise is lying. |
| "The cohorts measured aspartame." | Most measured *diet drinks*. Only NIH-AARP, NutriNet-Santé, CPS-II NC 2026 and the Nurses' Health Studies estimated aspartame in mg. |

## Numbers worth quoting on a website

- **NIH-AARP**: 473,984 adults (285,079 M / 188,905 F), aged 50–71, 1995–2000; 1,888 hematopoietic cancers, 315 gliomas. ≥600 mg/day: RR 0.98 (0.76–1.27). Glioma ≥400 mg/day: RR 0.73 (0.46–1.15).
- **NutriNet-Santé**: 102,865 adults, 78.5% women, mean age 42.2, median 7.7 y, 3,358 cancers, 2009–2021; higher-consumer mean aspartame **47.42 mg/day**. Total cancer HR 1.15 (1.03–1.28); breast 1.22 (1.01–1.48); obesity-related 1.15 (1.01–1.32); acesulfame-K total cancer 1.13 (1.01–1.26). Published 24 March 2022.
- **Nurses' Health Studies**: ~30 y, 10,814 invasive breast cancers; aspartame per 200 mg/day HR **1.00 (0.98–1.03)**. *JNCI* 2025;117:795–800.
- **CPS-II (mortality)**: 934,777 adults, enrolled 1982, followed to 2016, 135,093 cancer deaths; ASB–pancreatic cancer HR 1.11 (1.02–1.20); ASB–obesity-related null after BMI adjustment.
- **CPS-II Nutrition Cohort (incidence)**: 100,004 adults, 98% non-Hispanic White, mean age 69, FFQ 1999 & 2003; all cancers aHR 1.13 (1.08–1.18); obesity-related 1.17 (1.09–1.26); digestive 1.19 (1.06–1.33). **No dose-response.** Aspartame content used: 70–180 mg per 12-oz diet soda, 20 mg per tabletop packet.
- **ASB meta-analysis**: 17 cohorts, 2,821,922 participants, 34,050 cases; overall cancer RR 1.03 (0.96–1.11); leukaemia RR 1.35 (1.03–1.77), +15% per 355 mL/day; colorectal RR 0.78 (0.62–0.99).
- **Women's Health Initiative**: 98,786 postmenopausal women, median 20.9 y, 207 liver cancers; ASB liver cancer HR 1.17 (0.70–1.94) *ns*; SSB HR 1.85 (1.16–2.96). *JAMA*, 8 Aug 2023.
- **Jones 2022 pooled (NIH-AARP + PLCO)**: 553,874 participants; 506,389 non-diabetic (839 liver cancers) — no ASB association; 47,485 diabetic (221 liver cancers).
- **EPIC (Stepien 2016, as corrected 2024)**: 477,206 participants, 11.4 y; 191 HCC / 66 intrahepatic bile duct / 236 gallbladder; ASB per serving HR 1.06 (1.03–1.09), **n = 151 cases** (corrected from 101).
- **Schernhammer 2012**: 22 y; 1,324 NHL, 285 myelomas, 339 leukaemias; men ≥1 diet soda/day NHL RR 1.31 (1.01–1.72), myeloma RR 2.02 (1.20–3.40); sugar soda NHL in men RR 1.66 (1.10–2.51).
- **Gallus 2007**: 9,296 cases / 7,028 controls, Italy 1991–2004; ORs 0.71–1.62 for aspartame/other sweeteners; no association.
- **Absolute-risk framing**: ~4 extra cancer cases per 1,000 people over 8 years, if the NutriNet-Santé association were entirely causal.

## What I could NOT verify

- **Full text of the CPS-II Nutrition Cohort 2026 *J Nutr* paper.** Both the journal page and ScienceDirect returned HTTP 403. All figures above come from the abstract as indexed by search. Case count (21,356 cancers through June 2017) is carried over from `jecfa-iarc-2023.md`, not independently re-verified here.
- **Exact effect estimate and metric for Jones 2022's diabetic-subgroup ASB result** (HR 1.13, 1.02–1.25 as reported in `jecfa-iarc-2023.md`). I confirmed the cohort sizes and case counts and the *null* result in non-diabetics from the ScienceDirect abstract, but could not open the paper to confirm whether that HR is per-serving or high-vs-low.
- **NIH-AARP lymphoid neoplasms (2022, *J Nutr*)** — HTTP 403 on the journal page. Title, cohort and null direction confirmed; sample size, case counts and hazard ratios not verified.
- **Site-specific odds ratios in Gallus 2007.** PubMed gives only the ranges (saccharin 0.46–1.58; aspartame/other 0.71–1.62) and the ovarian saccharin OR 0.46 (0.29–0.74). Per-site aspartame ORs were not retrievable (publisher 403).
- **Gurney 1997 odds ratios.** Design (56 cases / 94 controls, children born 1981+) and the null conclusion are confirmed; the numeric ORs and CIs are not.
- **The exact wording and date of the Brigham and Women's Hospital statement** is consistent across Embargo Watch, NPR and NBC News, but I could not reach a hospital-hosted primary copy. The NBC News page returned 403; the NPR page timed out on fetch though it is indexed. Treat the quote as press-reported, not primary.
- **Whether any completed systematic review/meta-analysis of *aspartame specifically* (as opposed to artificially sweetened beverages) and cancer exists.** I found only a 2024 *protocol* in the Asian Pacific Journal of Environment and Cancer, and a 2025 *Frontiers in Medicine* umbrella meta-analysis whose contents I did not open. Gap.
- **Whether the CPS-II 2026 incidence result and the CPS-II 2022 mortality result have been reconciled by their authors** — they come from overlapping American Cancer Society cohorts and point different ways. I found no published commentary addressing this.
