# The "Nancy Markle" Chain Email, Betty Martini, and the Origin of Modern Aspartame Folklore

*Research brief. Compiled 2026-08-04. Primary artefacts and institutional statements prioritised; advocacy sources labelled inline. Companion to `efsa-2013-reevaluation.md`, `jecfa-iarc-2023.md`, `elsa-brasil-cognition-2025.md` — the toxicology and epidemiology are covered there and are not repeated here.*

## Bottom line

Nearly every viral aspartame claim still circulating in 2026 — that it causes multiple sclerosis, that MS is "really" methanol poisoning, that diet soda stored above 86°F turns into formaldehyde, that it caused Gulf War Syndrome, that it triggers lupus, blindness and seizures — can be traced to a single document written by one person: **Betty Martini** (1940–2023) of Duluth, Georgia, founder of the activist group **Mission Possible World Health International**. She posted it to Usenet in late 1995/early 1996 as a first-person account of "lecturing at the World Environmental Conference." From roughly December 1998 it circulated as a chain email under the byline **"Nancy Markle,"** a person for whom no trace has ever been found. Neither has any "World Environmental Conference" talk matching the letter's account.

The letter is not merely unsupported; its central factual anchor is documented as fabricated. The EPA scientist who actually gave the keynote Martini describes, **Dr. Jessica Gaylord**, wrote that she "did not make the quote you referenced… that comment was made by a woman in the audience during the Q & A session and her name was Betty Martini," and added, "I never used the word 'epidemic' because as a former NIH science administrator, I know better."

The honest caveats matter, though. The letter is not 100% invention: aspartame *does* yield methanol; PKU labelling *is* real; the 1980–81 FDA approval *was* genuinely contested; and some of the 1999-era debunkings themselves overclaimed in ways that have since been walked back (notably on weight control). A debunking site should say all of that.

## Key findings

**1. Authorship and byline are separable.** The chain email's text is substantially identical to messages Betty Martini posted to Usenet newsgroups in late 1995 and early 1996; the "Nancy Markle" byline was attached later. Martini's own account is that a post she wrote for a group of neurosurgeons was picked up by "Nancy Markle," who retitled it and added her own name. Either way, no Nancy Markle has ever been identified. A copy of Martini's own 1 Feb 1999 posting, signed "Betty Martini, Mission Possible International, 5950-H State Bridge Rd, Suite 215, Duluth GA 30097," survives in the `alt.health.cfs` archive. ([Usenet thread archive](https://groups.google.com/g/alt.health.cfs/c/MnMU13mzGmI); [Wikipedia, Aspartame controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspartame_controversy)) — **strong** for the text, **moderate** for who first attached the byline.

**2. The letter's factual foundation was denied by the person it quotes.** Gaylord's reply (reproduced in the same archived thread) is the single most damaging piece of evidence against the letter: the "EPA announced an epidemic of multiple sclerosis and systemic lupus" line — the letter's opening claim and its most-repeated one — is a misattribution of Martini's own audience question back onto the keynote speaker. ([Usenet thread archive](https://groups.google.com/g/alt.health.cfs/c/MnMU13mzGmI)) — **strong**.

**3. Verbatim claims, for citation.** From the archived text: "In the keynote address by the EPA, they announced that there was an epidemic of multiple sclerosis and systemic lupus"; "When the temperature of Aspartame exceeds 86 degrees F, the wood alcohol in ASPARTAME coverts to formaldehyde and then to formic acid"; "Aspartame Disease is partially the cause to what is behind some of the mystery of the Dessert Storm health problems… Several thousand pallets of diet drinks were shipped to the Dessert Storm troops"; "Memory loss is due to the fact that aspartic acid and phenylalanine are neurotoxic without the other amino acids found in protein"; "At the Conference of the American College of Physicians, doctors admitted that they did not know." (Spelling and capitalisation as in the original.) — **strong**.

**4. The MS organisations rebutted it directly, and one was named falsely.** The **Multiple Sclerosis Foundation** states flatly: "The MSF neither condemns nor endorses aspartame, and has never filed suit against the FDA" — a direct denial of a 1998 chain-letter claim that MSF was suing FDA to ban aspartame. Its Senior Medical Advisor **Dr. David Squillacote** denied any MS–aspartame link on **12 January 1999**. The UK **MS Trust** states: "There has been no published, peer-reviewed research that supports the link between aspartame and MS." The **National MS Society** lists it among disproven theories. ([MS Focus / MSF](https://msfocusmagazine.org/Magazine/Magazine-Items/Examining-the-Safety-of-Aspartame.aspx); [MS Trust](https://mstrust.org.uk/a-z/aspartame)) — **strong**.

**5. Lupus Foundation of America likewise.** LFA, via Dr. Evelyn Hess (chair of its Medical Council, a specialist in environmental influences on SLE), concluded there is "no specific proof of an association with aspartame as a cause or worsening of SLE," and states "We do not believe that aspartame leads to the development of lupus." ([LFA nutrition FAQs](https://www.lupus.org/resources/lupus-nutrition-faqs)) — **strong**.

**6. A university extension service published a point-by-point rebuttal.** University of Hawai'i at Mānoa CTAHR bulletin **FST-3, "Falsifications and Facts about Aspartame"** (Aurora Saulo Hodgson, Aug. 2001) addresses eight of Markle/Martini's claims by name — MS/lupus, methanol/formaldehyde/formate, phenylalanine and aspartic acid neurotoxicity, seizures, birth defects, cancer/brain tumours/headaches, appetite and weight, and "Desert Storm Syndrome" — and rejects each. It also records FDA's Dr. David Hattan (CFSAN Division of Health Effects Evaluation) stating there is no credible evidence aspartame elicits MS or systemic lupus. ([CTAHR FST-3 PDF](https://www.ctahr.hawaii.edu/oc/freepubs/pdf/fst-3.pdf)) — **strong**.

**7. Snopes rated it false.** Snopes' fact-check ("Is Aspartame Responsible for 'An Epidemic of Multiple Sclerosis and Lupus'?") dates the chain-letter form to 1998 and describes the letter as entirely false, misleading and defamatory. *Note: Snopes returned HTTP 402 to automated fetching in this session; the rating and the 1998 date are taken from search-index summaries and from Wikipedia's citation of the page, not from direct reading.* ([Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/aspartame-sweet-poison/)) — **moderate** (verdict well-attested, direct text not read).

**8. The "aspartame is misdiagnosed as MS" claim predates and outlived the letter, and was already answered by CDC.** CDC's 1984 investigation of 517 consumer complaints found "no specific constellation of symptoms clearly related to aspartame consumption." The peer-reviewed write-up (Bradstock et al., *Am J Clin Nutr* 1986;43(3):464–9) analysed 231 complaints and reported "no clear symptom complex that suggests a widespread public health hazard." ([PubMed 3953484](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3953484/); [UPI, 2 Nov 1984](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1984/11/02/CDC-says-aspartame-is-safe/4404468219600/)) — **strong**.

**9. The "tainted FDA approval" claim was independently audited — and the audit went against the letter.** GAO report **HRD-87-46**, "Food and Drug Administration: Food Additive Approval Process Followed for Aspartame" (issued 18 June 1987, released 17 July 1987), found the manufacturer submitted nearly 170 studies, that the studies CFSAN considered crucial met FDA requirements, and that CFSAN "adequately followed its food additive approval process." ([GAO HRD-87-46](https://www.gao.gov/products/hrd-87-46); [full PDF](https://www.gao.gov/assets/hrd-87-46.pdf)) — **strong** for what GAO found; see caveats for what GAO did *not* settle.

**10. Martini herself.** Betty (Geller) Martini, of Duluth GA, died 14 February 2023, aged 82. Her "Dr." title came from an honorary degree from an unaccredited institution, per McGill University's Office for Science and Society, which also records that no trace of Nancy Markle or the World Environmental Conference was ever found. ([Byrd & Flanigan obituary](https://www.byrdandflanigan.com/obituaries/betty-martini); [McGill OSS, Joe Schwarcz](https://www.mcgill.ca/oss/article/medical-critical-thinking-health-and-nutrition/aspartame-not-devil-incarnate-not-godsend-either)) — **strong**.

## Genuine caveats / what cuts the other way

- **The FDA approval history really was contested.** A 1980 FDA Public Board of Inquiry recommended withholding approval pending further study of brain-tumour findings; Commissioner Arthur Hull Hayes Jr. approved aspartame for dry foods on 15 July 1981; Donald Rumsfeld was CEO of G.D. Searle at the time; Hayes later joined Burson-Marsteller, a PR firm that worked for Searle. These facts are real and are why the conspiracy framing has traction. GAO 1987 concluded the *process* was followed — that is a procedural finding, not a declaration that no one had a conflict of interest. **Caution: most detailed narratives of the 1981 decision available online (including the widely-repeated "Hayes added a sixth panel member to break a 3–2 tie") come from activist sites, and I could not verify that specific mechanism from a primary document in this session.**
- **The letter is not chemically illiterate on every point.** Aspartame does release methanol (~10% by weight; roughly 18 mg in a can of diet soda per McGill OSS) and methanol is metabolised via formaldehyde to formate. The failure is dose and comparison, not chemistry: fruit juice, tomatoes and ordinary diet deliver more, and controlled feeding studies found no elevation in blood formate. See `efsa-2013-reevaluation.md` for the EFSA figures.
- **The 86°F claim is wrong in a specific, checkable way that debunkers often state imprecisely.** Heat does degrade aspartame — but into **diketopiperazine (DKP)** and constituent amino acids, losing sweetness; DKP has its own ADI (7.5 mg/kg bw/day, EFSA). Storage heat does not create formaldehyde in the can. Say "it degrades to DKP," not "nothing happens."
- **Some contemporaneous debunkings overclaimed and have aged badly.** CTAHR FST-3 (2001) asserts "no relationship between aspartame and increased appetite or weight gain" and that "aspartame products can help with weight control." WHO's 2023 conditional guideline on non-sugar sweeteners advises against using them for weight control. Do not reuse that 2001 line.
- **CDC's own 1986 paper conceded something.** Bradstock et al. explicitly noted "some case reports in which the symptoms may be attributable to aspartame in commonly-consumed amounts." That is a real, if narrow, concession from the debunking side.
- **Headache is the one symptom with positive controlled-trial data.** Van den Eeden et al., *Neurology* 1994;44(10):1787–93: double-blind four-period crossover, ~30 mg/kg/day, in 32 self-identified aspartame-sensitive volunteers — headaches on 33% of aspartame days vs 24% of placebo days (p = 0.04). Critics (Levy 1995; Schiffman 1995) noted one subject largely drove the difference, and only 18 of 32 completed. Weak but real, and it is the honest answer to "does anything in the letter have support?"
- **There is one published-abstract case report on MS.** Ross & Koffman (CMSC 2015 annual meeting) describe a single 35-year-old woman with a 13-year MS history who relapsed with four active MRI lesions after two weeks of heavy aspartame use. n = 1, conference abstract, no causal inference. Not evidence of a link — but "there is literally nothing in the literature" is an overstatement.
- **The "92 symptoms" / "75–80% of FDA additive complaints" figures are not fabricated.** FDA's Adverse Reaction Monitoring System really did log a large volume of aspartame complaints, and a 92-symptom list really was released. The error is inferential — voluntary reports about a heavily publicised product do not establish causation, which is exactly what CDC's investigation tested and did not find. **I could not retrieve the primary FDA ARMS document from a .gov URL in this session**; the figures circulate almost exclusively on activist sites.

## Myths in this area and what is actually true

| Claim in the letter | Status |
|---|---|
| "EPA announced an epidemic of MS and lupus" caused by aspartame | **False and fabricated.** The EPA keynote speaker says the words were Martini's own, from the audience. |
| Nancy Markle was a physician/researcher who lectured at a World Environmental Conference | **No such person or talk located**, by Snopes, McGill OSS, or anyone since 1998. |
| MS is often "really" methanol toxicity from aspartame | **False.** Rejected by MSF, MS Trust, National MS Society, FDA CFSAN. |
| Aspartame causes/worsens lupus | **False.** Rejected by Lupus Foundation of America. |
| MS Foundation was suing FDA to ban aspartame | **False.** MSF: "has never filed suit against the FDA." |
| Above 86°F aspartame converts to formaldehyde then formic acid | **False as stated.** Heat degradation yields DKP; formaldehyde is a transient metabolic intermediate, not a storage product. |
| Aspartame caused Gulf War / "Desert Storm" Syndrome | **Unsupported.** No connection found (CTAHR 2001). |
| Aspartame causes seizures | **Not supported.** Epilepsy Foundation of America and the Epilepsy Institute (NY) considered it safe for people with epilepsy (per CTAHR 2001). |
| Aspartame causes birth defects | **Not supported** at human exposures; see `efsa-2013-reevaluation.md` for EFSA's nuanced handling of animal developmental data. |
| Aspartame's approval was corruptly obtained | **Partly grounded, conclusion unsupported.** The 1980 PBOI dispute and Hayes/Rumsfeld/Burson-Marsteller facts are real; GAO 1987 found the approval process was followed. |

## What I could NOT verify

- **Direct text of the Snopes fact-check.** Snopes returned HTTP 402 to two fetch attempts. The "false / misleading and defamatory" wording and the 1998 first-appearance date come from search-engine summaries and Wikipedia's citation, not from reading the page. Verify before quoting verbatim.
- **Who first attached the "Nancy Markle" byline.** Martini's account (that a third party renamed her post) and the majority secondary account (that the byline is simply pseudonymous cover for her own text) cannot be adjudicated from available evidence. State the text's authorship as established; state the byline's origin as unresolved.
- **Any FDA .gov page.** Four attempts at FDA URLs (`aspartame-and-other-sweeteners-food`, the aspartame timeline page, `/media/89189/download`) returned 404 or were blocked.
