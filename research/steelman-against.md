# The Strongest Honest Case Against Aspartame

*Adversarial brief. Compiled 2026-08-05. Written deliberately against the site's thesis, to find what a well-informed critic could say that the site has no answer to. Advocacy sources labelled inline on both sides.*

## Bottom line

The strongest case against aspartame is **not** the cancer case. Cancer is where the critics are weakest: the Ramazzini lymphoma finding has never been independently replicated, the liver-cancer signal that drove IARC's 2B classification failed to replicate in a 1.5-million-person pooled analysis in 2026, and the Nurses' Health Studies demolished the NutriNet-Santé breast-cancer result at far greater power. A critic who leads with cancer will lose.

The strongest case is **cardiometabolic, and it is newer than most of this site's content**. It has three legs that now point the same way: (1) a 2025 *Cell Metabolism* paper showing aspartame triggers vagally-mediated insulin secretion in mice **and monkeys** and accelerates atherosclerosis through a specific, genetically-proven chemokine pathway, at a dose the authors put at ~3 cans of diet soda a day; (2) two large NutriNet-Santé analyses reporting aspartame associations with cerebrovascular disease and — strikingly — a **69% higher type 2 diabetes risk** for higher consumers of artificial sweeteners; (3) the fact that the ADI was never designed to detect this class of effect. The ADI is a classical toxicology construct — a 1981 rodent NOAEL divided by 100. It bounds organ toxicity and carcinogenicity. It does not bound chronic low-dose endocrine signalling, and no regulator has re-opened the file since 2013.

Layered on that is a governance argument that is genuinely unresolved: the two peer-reviewed Millstone & Dawson papers alleging asymmetric study appraisal by EFSA, EFSA's contested reply, and the fact that EFSA has apparently never formally responded to IARC's 2023 classification.

And the honest concession the other way: **the recent critical literature is mostly junk by volume.** Of ~46 aspartame papers indexed on PubMed for 2024–26 that report harm, a large majority are "network toxicology / molecular docking / machine learning" in-silico papers in which no animal or human is ever exposed to aspartame. They link aspartame to endometriosis, erectile dysfunction, oral leukoplakia, rheumatoid arthritis, kidney stones and Alzheimer's using the same computational template. A steelman must throw nearly all of them out.

## Key findings

**1. The single most damaging finding if replicated: aspartame → vagal activation → insulin → CX3CL1 → atherosclerosis.** Wu et al., *Cell Metabolism* 2025;37(5):1075–1088.e7 (Shandong University, Karolinska Institutet, Harvard, Fudan; senior author Yihai Cao). 0.15% aspartame in feed "markedly increased insulin secretion in mice **and monkeys**." Bilateral subdiaphragmatic vagotomy abolished the insulin rise, establishing a parasympathetic mechanism. In ApoE−/− mice, aspartame feeding aggravated plaque formation via an insulin-dependent mechanism; an implanted insulin slow-release pump reproduced the effect; and deleting *Cx3cr1* in monocytes/macrophages **completely abrogated** it. Press materials from the authors put 0.15% at roughly three cans of diet soda per day for a human — i.e. **inside the ADI**. ([Cell Metabolism, 6 May 2025](https://www.cell.com/cell-metabolism/fulltext/S1550-4131(25)00006-3); [PMID 39978336](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39978336/); [Karolinska Institutet news](https://news.ki.se/how-aspartame-affects-blood-vessels-in-mice)) — **moderate** as evidence about humans, **strong** as a mechanistic result in animals. This is the finding to watch.

**2. Why finding 1 matters more than any cancer study.** JECFA's central mechanistic defence is that aspartame is fully hydrolysed in the gut, "no aspartame enters the systemic circulation," and plasma metabolites do not rise at ADI doses. A cephalic/vagal insulin response requires **no systemic aspartame at all** — it is triggered by sweet taste and gut signalling upstream of absorption. If it holds in humans, the hydrolysis argument stops being a complete answer, and it does so without contradicting a single toxicology finding. That is what makes it dangerous to the consensus.

**3. NutriNet-Santé: type 2 diabetes.** 105,588 French adults, 2009–2022, 946,650 person-years, **972 incident T2D cases**. Higher consumers (above sex-specific medians of 16.4–18.5 mg/day) vs non-consumers: total artificial sweeteners **HR 1.69 (95% CI 1.45–1.97)**; **aspartame HR 1.63 (1.38–1.93)**; acesulfame-K 1.70 (1.42–2.04); sucralose 1.34 (1.07–1.69). Adjusted for weight change during follow-up. Authors: the findings "strengthen the evidence that these additives may not be safe sugar alternatives." ([Debras et al., *Diabetes Care* 2023;46(9):1681–90, PMID 37490630](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37490630/)) — **moderate** as an association, **weak** as causal evidence (see caveats).

**4. NutriNet-Santé: cardiovascular disease.** 103,388 participants, 904,206 person-years, **1,502 CVD events**. Total artificial sweeteners **HR 1.09 (1.01–1.18, P=0.03)**; **aspartame–cerebrovascular events HR 1.17 (1.03–1.33, P=0.02)** (186 vs 151 events per 100,000 person-years); acesulfame-K–CHD 1.40 (1.06–1.84); sucralose–CHD 1.31 (1.00–1.71). ([Debras et al., *BMJ* 2022;378:e071204, 7 Sep 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36638072/); [PMC9593923](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9593923/)) — **moderate**. Independent of industry funding.

**5. The ADI answers the wrong question.** The 0–40 mg/kg bw/day ADI comes from a 4,000 mg/kg bw/day NOAEL in a 104-week rat feeding study (Ishii 1981, a manufacturer study) divided by a 100-fold factor, set at JECFA's 25th meeting in **1981** and never revised in 45 years. The 100× factor covers interspecies and interindividual **toxicokinetic and toxicodynamic** variation for classical toxicity endpoints. It was not constructed to bound receptor-mediated endocrine signalling, microbiome effects, or transgenerational epigenetic effects — none of which behave monotonically with dose in the way NOAEL/uncertainty-factor logic assumes. A critic does not need to allege that the ADI is *wrong*; only that it is **not responsive** to the modern hypotheses. That is a stronger and much harder-to-rebut position.

**6. The regulator-methodology critique is a two-paper exchange the site treats as one.** Millstone & Dawson, *Archives of Public Health* 2019;77:34, reported that of 154 studies EFSA appraised, **all 73** indicating possible harm were judged unreliable, while 62 of 81 showing no harm were judged reliable. EFSA staff Kass & Lodi replied (2 April 2020) with a table giving different denominators: **78 "negative" studies (51 reliable / 27 unreliable) and 37 "positive" studies (21 reliable / 16 unreliable)** — arguing EFSA had found over a third of negatives unreliable and over half of positives reliable. ([Kass & Lodi, PMC7114778](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7114778/)) Millstone & Dawson then published a **direct rejoinder** — "Why did EFSA not reduce its ADI for aspartame or recommend its use should no longer be permitted?", *Archives of Public Health*, November 2020 ([doi:10.1186/s13690-020-00489-w](https://archpublichealth.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13690-020-00489-w)) — and continue to call for suspension of EU authorisation. Millstone is emeritus professor of science policy at Sussex; this is peer-reviewed academic criticism, not activism. — **strong** that the exchange exists and is unresolved; **contested** on the merits.

**7. The exposure-misclassification argument cuts against the site, not for it.** The site's standard move against positive cohorts is "they measured artificially sweetened beverages, not aspartame." That is true — and it is a **non-differential** misclassification, which in the general case biases estimates **toward the null**. So the same objection implies the true aspartame-specific effect could be *larger* than the published HRs, not smaller. Using misclassification only when it helps is the site's most exploitable asymmetry. — **strong** as a methodological point.

**8. Ramazzini is not settled, and the recent movement is toward the critics.** Landrigan, Straif and colleagues, "Irregularities in the IARC Working Group Evaluation of Ramazzini Institute Aspartame Studies," *Annals of Global Health*, 6 June 2025, argue IARC's animal-evidence assessment was superficial and should have been *stronger*, and correct at least one IARC criticism of the study design. Tibaldi et al. (2020) re-read all 78 haematopoietic/lymphoid tumour cases with a nine-marker immunohistochemistry panel and confirmed 72. Neither is independent of the originating institute or its allies, but "the Ramazzini findings were refuted" is not a defensible sentence. (Detail in `ramazzini-rodent-studies.md`.)

**9. Two non-cancer animal findings at real-world doses, both in serious venues, both contested.** (a) Jones et al., *PNAS* 2022;119(49):e2213120119 (Florida State, Bhide lab): aspartame at doses equivalent to **below 15% of the FDA maximum** produced anxiety-like behaviour in mice, transmitted to two generations through the paternal line, with glutamate–GABA and amygdala gene-expression changes. A critique by Charles Vorhees and an author reply were both published in *PNAS* in 2023 — the design and analysis are genuinely disputed. (b) Choudhary & Singh, *Toxicology Mechanisms and Methods*, 14 May 2026 ([PMID 42010990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42010990/)): meta-analysis of 13 rodent studies, pooled oxidative-damage effect size **+4.44**, with neurobehavioural and glucose-intolerance findings "even at doses matching human acceptable daily intake levels" — but **I² = 85.4%** and the authors themselves flag incomplete reporting. — **weak-to-moderate**; do not quote the +4.44 without the heterogeneity.

**10. The precautionary argument is stronger here than in most food-safety disputes, because the benefit side is nearly empty.** Aspartame has no nutritional value; the realistic counterfactual is water; and the best RCT evidence puts the weight benefit of substituting sweetened for sugar-sweetened beverages at about **1 kg** (McGlynn 2022: −1.06 kg, 95% CI −1.71 to −0.41). When expected benefit is that small, even a modest probability of a chronic cardiometabolic harm dominates the expected-value calculation. Three public bodies have already acted on something like this reasoning — WHO (15 May 2023, conditional recommendation against NSS for weight control), the UK SACN (2025) and the US Dietary Guidelines (Jan 2026). A critic can say, correctly: *the public-health establishment already agrees with me on the recommendation; you are arguing with them, not with me.*

## Genuine caveats / what cuts the other way

- **The 2024–26 "harm" literature is inflated by an in-silico paper genre.** A PubMed sweep of 2024–26 aspartame papers reporting harm returns dozens using the same template — network toxicology + molecular docking + machine learning + single-cell reanalysis — linking aspartame to PCOS, endometriosis, erectile dysfunction, oral leukoplakia, rheumatoid arthritis, ulcerative colitis, coronary heart disease, kidney stones, renal papillary carcinoma, Alzheimer's and ovarian cancer. **No aspartame is administered to anything in these studies.** They are hypothesis-generating pattern-matching against public databases. Their existence is not evidence, and a critic who cites paper *counts* is being dishonest. This is probably the most useful single thing this brief contributes to the site.
- **The Cell Metabolism study's weaknesses are real and were stated publicly by named experts.** Science Media Centre expert reaction, 19 Feb 2025: Prof Oliver Jones (RMIT) — the mice were genetically susceptible ApoE−/− animals on a high-fat diet, actual aspartame consumption was not measured, and "aspartame doesn't stimulate glucose or insulin levels in humans." Prof Naveed Sattar (Glasgow) — "before it can be taken seriously, the findings have to be replicated in man," and he continues to prefer diet drinks to sugar-sweetened ones. Prof James Leiper (BHF) — "these findings have not yet been seen in humans." ([Science Media Centre, 19 Feb 2025](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-study-looking-at-aspartame-artificial-sweetener-and-insulin-levels-and-blood-vessel-inflammation-in-mice/))
- **HR 1.63 for diabetes at ~17 mg/day is biologically implausible on its face.** That is under a tenth of one diet soda, ~1/100th of the ADI. An effect that large at an exposure that small, with no coherent dose-response, is the classic signature of residual confounding and reverse causality — people switch to sweeteners *because* they are pre-diabetic. The authors themselves concede reverse causality cannot be excluded. The same objection applies to the CVD paper.
- **NutriNet-Santé's structural limits.** ~79% women, self-selected web volunteers, mean age 42, French diet. It is one cohort. It has produced positive findings for cancer, CVD and diabetes; that internal consistency is as easily read as a shared cohort-level bias as a real effect.
- **The cancer leg of the critical case has been getting weaker, not stronger.** The 2026 pooled analysis of 11 cohorts (1,518,411 participants, 2,811 liver cancers) found "little evidence" of an ASB–liver-cancer association; the Nurses' Health Studies (10,814 invasive breast cancers) found HR 1.00 (0.98–1.03) per 200 mg/day aspartame. A critic who has to defend IARC's 2B basis is defending ground that has been eroding since 2023.
- **The best-designed human sweetener/microbiome trial is not helpful to the critics** — see "what I could not verify."
- **Source hygiene, both directions.** On the critical side: US Right To Know, Mission Possible/Betty Martini, the Feingold Association and Yuka are **advocacy**, and Ralph Walton's funding survey is unpublished and non-peer-reviewed — none should be cited. On the defence side: the Calorie Control Council, International Sweeteners Association, IAFNS/ILSI, the American Beverage Association and Ajinomoto have funded a substantial share of the published *criticism* of the Ramazzini work and of the sweetener/weight RCT literature (Haighton, Roberts, Elmore, Rogers, Drewnowski). A steelman is entitled to point out that the rebuttals are not neutral either.

## Myths in this area and what is actually true

| Critic's claim | What's actually true |
|---|---|
| "Hundreds of new studies show aspartame causes disease X." | Most 2024–26 "harm" papers are in-silico network-toxicology exercises with zero exposure. Count experiments, not PMIDs. |
| "IARC says aspartame causes cancer." | IARC said *possibly*, on limited evidence, on a hazard basis; the liver signal that drove it has since failed to replicate at scale. |
| "The ADI has never been tested." | It has been re-derived independently by JECFA (2023) and EFSA (2013). The defensible criticism is that it is not *responsive* to cardiometabolic hypotheses, not that it is untested. |
| "All aspartame research is industry-funded." | False; IARC's 2025 mapping of 619 aspartame studies found ~10% disclosed industry relationships. The honest version is that a disproportionate share of the *rebuttal* literature is. |
| "Regulators refuse to look at this." | EFSA published 112 original industry studies in 2011 and a 263-page review in 2013. The defensible criticism is that nothing has been re-opened **since**. |

## Corrections to the existing site

1. **`caveats.html` — the Millstone/EFSA exchange is described as ending without a reply.** The page says the two sides "count different study sets — 154 versus 37-plus — and neither published a reconciliation." Millstone & Dawson **did** publish a direct rejoinder to Kass & Lodi: *"Why did EFSA not reduce its ADI for aspartame or recommend its use should no longer be permitted?"*, *Archives of Public Health*, November 2020, doi:10.1186/s13690-020-00489-w. The site should cite it. (The site's characterisation of EFSA's counts is accurate — Kass & Lodi's Table 1 gives 78 negatives / 51 reliable and 37 positives / 21 reliable.)

2. **No page on the site carries the NutriNet-Santé cardiovascular or type 2 diabetes analyses.** `index.html` gestures at "diabetes, cardiovascular signals," and `cancer.html` and `caveats.html` cover the 2022 *PLOS Medicine* cancer paper — but the *BMJ* 2022 CVD paper (aspartame–cerebrovascular HR 1.17) and the *Diabetes Care* 2023 T2D paper (aspartame HR 1.63) appear nowhere. These are the two most-cited anti-aspartame human studies after the cancer paper. A critic will raise them, and the site currently has no answer on the page. **This is the largest single gap.**

3. **The site has no coverage of Wu et al., *Cell Metabolism* 2025.** It is the most methodologically serious recent paper reporting harm, in one of the highest-impact journals in metabolism, with cross-species (mouse + monkey) insulin data and a genetically proven mechanism, at a dose the authors put inside the ADI. It also partially undercuts the "fully hydrolysed, nothing enters circulation" argument that appears on `metabolism.html` and `the-evidence.html`, because a vagal/cephalic insulin response does not require systemic aspartame. This needs a section, with the Science Media Centre criticisms attached.

4. **`the-evidence.html` uses the "artificially sweetened beverages ≠ aspartame" argument only in one direction.** Non-differential exposure misclassification biases toward the null. The page should either drop the argument or state that it implies the true effect could be larger as well as smaller. `cancer-cohort-studies.md` already gets this right ("an inference, not a measurement — in both directions"); the published page does not.

5. **Verification item, not yet a correction:** `the-evidence.html` describes 4,000 mg/kg bw/day as a NOAEL "100× the ADI." EFSA's own wording is that the studies "revealed no aspartame-related increase in any type of neoplasms **at all doses tested**," which suggests 4,000 mg/kg was the **highest dose tested** rather than a NOAEL with an observed effect above it. If so, it is a limit-dose, and the phrasing overstates how much is known about the region above it. Worth checking against the EFSA opinion text before the next edit.

## What I could NOT verify

- **The full text of Millstone & Dawson's 2020 rejoinder.** Springer/BioMed Central returned an authentication redirect on three attempts. I verified the paper's existence, exact title, journal, month (Nov 2020) and DOI, and its framing (a response to Kass & Lodi restating that EFSA deemed all 73 harm-indicating studies unreliable), but I did **not** read their point-by-point reply to EFSA's 37/78 denominators. The site should not characterise their rebuttal's contents until someone reads it.
- **Whether the Cell Metabolism "≈3 cans of diet soda" conversion appears in the paper itself or only in press materials.** I found it in the Karolinska/EurekAlert releases and secondary coverage; the paper's own text states only "0.15%." Attribute it as an author press-office figure, not a published dose conversion, until the methods section is checked.
- **The Suez/Elinav 2022 *Cell* human sweetener RCT result for aspartame specifically.** I did not have search budget to re-verify it in this session. My recollection is that aspartame was among the sweeteners that did **not** significantly impair glycaemic response in that trial, which would be a strong counterweight to the insulin hypothesis — but the site must not publish that until it is checked. This is the highest-value single verification left in this area.
- **The 2026 *Allergy* paper on aspartame and gut epithelial barrier damage (PMID 41137210) and the 2026 *Respiratory Research* asthma paper (PMID 42464261).** Both appear to involve real experimental exposure rather than in-silico modelling, and both are in credible journals, but I did not read the abstracts or doses. They may belong in a future gut-microbiome brief.
- **Whether Ishii 1981's 4,000 mg/kg bw/day was the highest dose tested** (see correction item 5). I inferred this from EFSA's "at all doses tested" phrasing but did not confirm it against the study or the EFSA opinion's dose tables.
- **Any 2024–26 formal EFSA, FDA or JECFA response to the Cell Metabolism 2025 paper.** I found none, but did not search specifically for one.
