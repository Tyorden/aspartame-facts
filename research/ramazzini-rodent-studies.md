# The Ramazzini Institute rodent studies: the source of the aspartame–cancer alarm

*Companion to `efsa-2013-reevaluation.md` (which covers EFSA's rejection at summary level) and `jecfa-iarc-2023.md` (which covers the 2023 hazard/risk split). This brief goes into the primary rodent data and the pathology dispute itself.*

## Bottom line

Essentially every "aspartame causes cancer" headline traces back to three lifetime bioassays from one laboratory — the European Ramazzini Foundation / Ramazzini Institute (RI) in Bologna, published 2005–2010 by Morando Soffritti and colleagues. Those studies did report statistically significant, dose-related increases in lymphomas/leukaemias, at doses reaching down to roughly the human ADI. That is real and should not be waved away. But the central diagnostic finding — lymphoma — is exactly the diagnosis that an independent NTP/EPA-sponsored pathology re-read of *other* RI studies found the institute's pathologists most often called wrong, because the rat colony carried chronic pulmonary infection whose inflammatory infiltrates mimic lymphoma. FDA could not complete its review because full data were not supplied; EFSA rejected the findings in 2006, twice in 2009, and again in 2013; EPA formally decided in 2010 not to use RI lymphoma/leukaemia data in IRIS assessments at all. The most defensible position is not "the Ramazzini studies were fraudulent" but "the one tumour type they hang on is the one the laboratory's conditions made least reliable, and no independent laboratory has reproduced it."

## Key findings

**1. The 2005/2006 rat study: what it actually reported.** Sprague-Dawley rats, 100–150 per sex per group, dosed in feed *ad libitum* from 8 weeks of age **until spontaneous death**, at 100,000 / 50,000 / 10,000 / 2,000 / 400 / 80 / 0 ppm (≈5,000 / 2,500 / 500 / 100 / 20 / 4 mg/kg bw/day). Lymphomas+leukaemias in **females**: control 13/150 (8.7%), and 22/150 (14.7%, ns), 30/150 (20.0%, p≤0.01), 28/150 (18.7%, p≤0.05), 19/100 (19.0%, p≤0.05), 25/100 (25.0%, p≤0.01), 25/100 (25.0%, p≤0.01) at 80→100,000 ppm. Dose-trend p≤0.05. ([Soffritti et al., *Eur J Oncol* 2005, full text PDF](https://www.ramazzini.org/wp-content/uploads/2008/03/Aspartame-induces-lymphomas-and-leukaemias-in-rats_2005.pdf); expanded as [*Environ Health Perspect* 114:379, 2006](https://ehp.niehs.nih.gov/doi/10.1289/ehp.8711)) — **strong** as a description of what was published.

**2. The effect was female-only, and the male data undercut it.** From the same Table 2: male controls had **31/150 (20.7%)** lymphomas/leukaemias — more than double the female control rate (8.7%), and *higher* than four of the six treated male groups. No male dose group reached significance. So the "increase" in females is partly an artefact of an unusually low female control value against a colony background that, in males, ran at 20%. (My arithmetic directly from the published table.) — **strong** (arithmetic), **moderate** as an interpretation.

**3. The 2007 prenatal rat study and the 2010 mouse study.** 2007: 70–95 rats/sex/group at 2,000, 400, 0 ppm from gestational day 12 to natural death; significant dose-related increase in malignant-tumour-bearing males (p<0.01) and in lymphomas/leukaemias. ([Soffritti et al., *EHP* 115:1293, 2007](https://ehp.niehs.nih.gov/doi/abs/10.1289/ehp.10271)) 2010: Swiss mice, prenatal-through-lifespan feed; significant dose-related hepatocellular carcinoma in **males** (p<0.01; individually significant at 32,000 ppm p<0.01 and 16,000 ppm p<0.05) and alveolar/bronchiolar carcinoma in males (p<0.05); **nothing in females**. ([Soffritti et al., *Am J Ind Med* 53:1197, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20886530/)) — **strong** as description. Note the sex-inconsistency across studies: females drove the rat signal, males the mouse signal.

**4. The decisive independent evidence is a pathology re-read — of RI's *other* studies, not aspartame.** In April 2010 NTP pathologists visited RI; in 2011 NTP and EPA sponsored an independent Pathology Working Group review by Experimental Pathology Laboratories covering RI studies of methanol, MTBE, ETBE, vinyl chloride and acrylonitrile — **13,011 slides from 800 rats** (methanol, three QA pathologists) and **6,751 slides from 360 rats** (MTBE). For solid tumours (e.g. liver tumours in the vinyl chloride study) there was **good agreement**. For lymphoma there was not: in the methanol study the PWG found **no treatment-related increase**, and specific counts diverged sharply (e.g. male controls: RI 26 lymphomas vs PWG 13; high-dose females: RI 25 vs PWG 6). The PWG attributed the gap to difficulty distinguishing lymphoma from **concurrent lung infection and inflammatory infiltrates** in an infected ageing colony. ([Gift et al., *Environ Health Perspect* 2013, US EPA authors](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3852791/)) — **strong**.

**5. EPA acted on it formally.** EPA decided **not to rely on RI lymphoma and leukaemia data** in IRIS assessments (methanol, MTBE, ETBE), while continuing to accept RI solid-tumour data, and asked RI to implement the PWG's laboratory recommendations. ([US EPA, "Update on Ramazzini Institute Data in IRIS Assessments"](https://www.epa.gov/iris/update-ramazzini-institute-data-iris-assessments)) — **strong**. This is the single most quotable regulator action, because it is narrow, reasoned, and *not* a blanket dismissal of the institute.

**6. FDA's 2007 review was incomplete because data were withheld.** On 20 April 2007 FDA stated the ERF data made available to it "do not appear to support the aspartame-related findings reported by ERF" and "do not provide evidence to alter FDA's conclusion that the use of aspartame is safe," and that the histopathological changes reported appeared incidental/spontaneous rather than treatment-related. FDA also stated it **could not conduct a complete and definitive review** because ERF did not provide the full study data; FDA had requested the remaining data in June 2006 and offered to review pathology slides. ([FDA Statement on European Aspartame Study, 20 Apr 2007, via EPA HERO record](https://hero.epa.gov/reference/625358/); contemporaneous trade coverage: [BakeryAndSnacks, 23 Apr 2007](https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2007/04/23/fda-reviews-italian-aspartame-study/)) — **strong** for the quoted wording; **moderate** for the precise sequence of requests.

**7. EFSA's grounds, stated four times.** 2006 (44-page AFC opinion), 2009 (EFSA Journal 945), 2009 updated after ERF supplied data in February 2009 (EFSA Journal 1015), 2013 (full re-evaluation). Core reasoning: the lymphoma/leukaemia increase "was unrelated to aspartame, given the high background incidence of chronic inflammatory changes in the lungs and the lack of a positive dose-response relationship"; renal-pelvis tumours were a high-dose, rat-specific effect of no human relevance; schwannoma numbers were low with a flat dose-response and diagnostic uncertainty; aggregating all malignant tumours for statistics was not justified; the 2010 mouse tumour incidences fell within RI's **own** historical control range. ADI unchanged at 40 mg/kg bw/day throughout. ([EFSA aspartame topic page](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/aspartame); [EFSA 2013 draft opinion §94–105](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/consultation/130108.pdf)) — **strong**.

**8. The negative rodent literature is substantial.** JECFA reviewed 12 oral carcinogenicity studies; all but the four RI studies were negative (see `jecfa-iarc-2023.md`). NTP's own 9-month feed studies in three genetically modified mouse strains (Tg.AC hemizygous, p53 haploinsufficient, Cdkn2a-deficient), 15/sex/group at 0–50,000 ppm (up to ~7,660 mg/kg bw/day in males), found "no neoplasms or nonneoplastic lesions attributed to exposure to aspartame" in the Tg.AC and p53 models and "no evidence of carcinogenic activity" in p53 mice — with the honest caveat that NTP itself flagged uncertainty about these models' sensitivity. ([NTP GMM report, PMID 18685711](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18685711/)) — **strong**.

**9. NCI's epidemiological counterweight.** The NIH-AARP cohort (Lim et al. 2006) found higher aspartame-beverage consumption was not associated with lymphoma, leukaemia or brain cancer — i.e. the exact tumour types RI reported were looked for in humans at scale and not found. ([NCI Artificial Sweeteners and Cancer fact sheet](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/diet/artificial-sweeteners-fact-sheet)) — **strong**.

## Genuine caveats / what cuts the other way

- **The PWG never re-read the aspartame slides.** The 2011 PWG reviewed methanol, MTBE, ETBE, vinyl chloride and acrylonitrile. Extending its conclusions to the aspartame studies is an inference about a shared laboratory and colony, not a direct finding. Anyone who says "the aspartame slides were re-read and the tumours disappeared" is overclaiming.
- **RI did engage, contrary to the "they refused to share anything" line.** ERF submitted study data to EFSA in **February 2009**, prompting a second EFSA opinion that year. The accurate statement is that FDA judged the material it received incomplete — not that RI stonewalled everyone.
- **RI has since re-examined its own material with immunohistochemistry.** Tibaldi et al. (2020, *Acta Histochemica* 122:151548) re-read all **78** haematopoietic/lymphoid tumour cases from the prenatal rat study with a panel including Ki67, CD3, PAX5, CD20, CD68, TdT, CD45, CD14, CD33; **72** were confirmed as tumours, **3** reclassified as lymphoid hyperplasia, **3** as inflammatory lesions. That is a real, non-trivial answer to the "it was all inflammation" charge — though the re-read was done by the originating institute, not an independent panel. ([ScienceDirect record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0065128120300477))
- **Gnudi et al. 2023 restated the findings across all three bioassays** (BT6008: 2,270 rats; BT6009: ~330 rats; BT6010: 852 Swiss mice) using Fisher's exact and Cochran-Armitage trend tests. It is a re-analysis by RI authors, not new independent data. ([*Annals of Global Health*, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10289045/))
- **IARC's 2023 Working Group did find "limited evidence in experimental animals"** — a positive, if weak, finding, and it rested largely on the RI data. In June 2025 Landrigan, Straif and colleagues published "Irregularities in the IARC Working Group Evaluation of Ramazzini Institute Aspartame Studies," arguing IARC's animal assessment was superficial and should have been stronger, and correcting at least one IARC criticism (the rat study *did* limit one male and one female pup per litter). ([*Annals of Global Health*, 6 Jun 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12143253/)) The dispute is live, not closed.
- **Much of the published criticism carries industry money.** Haighton et al.'s quality-scoring review — which assigned Klimisch **2** ("reliable with restrictions") to the three Searle and three NTP studies and Klimisch **3** ("not reliable") to the three RI studies — was partly funded by the **Calorie Control Council** (industry). Roberts' 2021 rebuttal to Landrigan & Straif was funded by the same body. Elmore et al.'s two pathology critiques disclose that the lead author was compensated by the **American Beverage Association**. Abegaz's 2007 EHP letter "Aspartame Not Linked to Cancer" was written by an **Ajinomoto** (aspartame manufacturer) employee. Their technical arguments may still be right — the NTP/EPA PWG evidence is independent and reaches the same place — but none of these should ever be the sole support for a claim on a debunking site.
- **Lifetime-to-natural-death dosing is a design *choice*, not automatically a flaw.** RI's rationale is that standard 104-week studies terminate animals before the highest-incidence period of life. Critics counter that it inflates spontaneous tumour background and autolysis in found-dead animals. Both are true; this is a legitimate methodological disagreement, not a settled error.

## Myths in this area and what is actually true

- **Myth: "A study found aspartame causes leukaemia at doses below the ADI, and regulators buried it."** Regulators did not bury it — FDA, EFSA (four times), EPA, JECFA and IARC all examined it in public documents. What they concluded is that the lymphoma diagnoses are unreliable in this colony.
- **Myth: "Ramazzini refused to share their slides."** Partly false. ERF supplied data to EFSA in February 2009. FDA's complaint is specifically that what it received was not the full dataset and that its offer to review slides was not taken up.
- **Myth: "Every regulator says the Ramazzini work is worthless."** False and dangerous to repeat. EPA explicitly kept using RI **solid-tumour** data (it agreed well with the PWG on vinyl-chloride liver tumours) and said it hoped to use future RI data once lab procedures improved. The rejection is targeted at lymphoma/leukaemia diagnoses.
- **Myth (the other direction): "The Ramazzini findings were confirmed by independent scientists."** Not established. No independent laboratory has reproduced the lymphoma/leukaemia finding. The 2020 IHC re-read and the 2023 re-analysis were both done by the Ramazzini Institute itself.

## What I could NOT verify

- **Exact PWG concordance percentages.** A widely repeated set of figures ("NTP agreed with RI diagnoses for only 36% of all lymphomas, 21% of lymphomas affecting lung plus another organ, and none of the lung-only lymphomas") appears in secondary summaries. I could **not** locate those percentages in the text of Gift et al. 2013 (EHP) that I retrieved. The Table 2 counts I quote above **are** verified. Do not publish the 36%/21% figures without tracing them to the primary EPA/NTP PWG report.
- **Whether *Mycoplasma pulmonis* was specifically identified in the aspartame colonies.** Elmore et al. assert *M. pulmonis* contamination of RI colonies; Roberts (2021) states that RI's claim of "no mycoplasma infection detected" in the rat study was unsupported by presented evidence. Gift et al. 2013 refers only to "end-of-life infections" without naming an organism. The specific pathogen in the *aspartame* studies is unresolved in the sources I read.
- **The full text of EFSA Journal 945 and 1015.** Wiley and the mirror hosts returned 403/CAPTCHA. My characterisation of EFSA's 2009 reasoning rests on EFSA's own topic page, the 2013 draft opinion, and secondary quotation. The direct quotes attributed to EFSA above should be re-checked against the original PDFs before publication.
- **The 2006 EHP paper's own text.** I verified the 2005 *European Journal of Oncology* version (which carries the same Table 2 data) directly; the EHP 2006 version I did not open. Incidence figures cited are from the 2005 paper.
- **Whether RI ever offered slides to FDA after 2007**, or whether the IARC 2023 Working Group obtained original RI slides. Not established in any source I found.
- **Any post-2025 independent replication attempt.** I found none. If one exists it is outside the sources retrieved here.
