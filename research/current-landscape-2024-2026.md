# Current regulatory and political landscape, 2024 – August 2026

*Research brief. Written 2026-08-05. Scope: regulatory action, legislation, political attention, corporate
behaviour and 2025–26 studies not covered by the other briefs in this directory. Cancer cohorts are in
`cancer-cohort-studies.md`; IARC/JECFA in `jecfa-iarc-2023.md`; the WHO/SACN/DGA guideline fight in
`weight-obesity-rcts.md`; the Neurology cognition study in `elsa-brasil-cognition-2025.md`.*

## Bottom line

Between January 2024 and August 2026 aspartame attracted a great deal of political attention and almost no
regulatory action. It is still approved everywhere it was approved in 2023, at the same ADIs. The FDA built a
brand-new post-market chemical review programme and did **not** put aspartame in it. Federal MAHA documents
name aspartame as a "potential concern" but recommend nothing specific. The single genuinely consequential
pending item is European: EFSA committed to updating the aspartame safety assessment — including whether the
40 mg/kg ADI needs revising — inside its re-evaluation of the aspartame–acesulfame salt (E 962), promised for
end-2025, and as of today it is still listed as ongoing. At state level exactly one US law names aspartame:
Louisiana. And the 2025–26 research crop contains one animal paper that is genuinely awkward for a safety
site, because its effects appeared at roughly one-sixth of the human ADI.

## Key findings

**1. Louisiana is the only US jurisdiction whose enacted law names aspartame.** Louisiana SB 14 (2025 Regular
Session) became **Act 463**, signed **20 June 2025**. Two separate mechanisms: (a) aspartame is item (4) of a
**44-ingredient** list triggering a **QR-code disclosure** on the package linking to a manufacturer-controlled
page carrying the words *"NOTICE: This product contains [ingredient]. For more information about this
ingredient, including FDA approvals, click HERE"* — effective **1 January 2028**; and (b) aspartame is item
(14) of a **15-item** "prohibited ingredient" list banned from public and state-funded nonpublic school meals
from the **2028–29 school year**. Acesulfame potassium and sucralose are on both lists.
([Enrolled bill text, legis.la.gov](https://legis.la.gov/legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1421347)) — **strong
(primary text, read directly)**

**2. The bigger state laws deliberately left aspartame off.** Texas SB 25 (signed 18 June 2025) imposes the
"not recommended for human consumption by the appropriate authority in Australia, Canada, the EU or the UK"
warning label on **44** ingredients from 1 January 2027 — dyes, BHA, BHT, potassium bromate, titanium dioxide,
propylparaben, partially hydrogenated oil — and **no sweeteners**. West Virginia HB 2354, the first
retail-plus-school additive restriction, covers seven dyes, BHA and propylparaben and **not** aspartame.
Arkansas, Utah and Virginia likewise cover dyes and preservatives only.
([Food Processing, state ban tracker, 12 Jan 2026](https://www.foodprocessing.com/ingredients/article/55342021/keeping-track-of-state-ingredient-bans);
[Morrison Foerster, 1 Jul 2025](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250701-newly-enacted-food-ingredient-disclosure-laws))
— **moderate-to-strong (two independent secondary trackers; Louisiana confirmed against primary text)**

**3. California AB 1264 reaches aspartame by category, not by name.** Signed **9 October 2025**, it phases
"particularly harmful ultraprocessed food" out of K-12 school meals by **2035**, and lists **non-nutritive
sweeteners** among the ingredient categories the California Department of Public Health must use in defining
that term. Which specific sweeteners get named is a rulemaking question that has not been answered yet.
([CalMatters, Oct 2025](https://calmatters.org/health/2025/10/ultra-processed-food-school-meals-law/)) —
**moderate**

**4. The MAHA assessment named aspartame — and asked for nothing.** *The MAHA Report: Make Our Children
Healthy Again Assessment*, **22 May 2025**, lists "certain artificial sweeteners (e.g., aspartame, sucralose,
and saccharin)" among additives of potential concern, with the specific claim that they "have been observed to
interfere with the gut microbiome in some studies." It recommends no regulatory action on them. The follow-up
MAHA *Strategy* report (September 2025) did not carry sweeteners forward — a gap noticed and criticised by
outside nutrition researchers.
([Covington & Burling summary, May 2025](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/05/maha-commission-releases-make-our-children-healthy-again-assessment);
[STAT op-ed, 11 Sep 2025](https://www.statnews.com/2025/09/11/maha-healthy-kids-non-sugar-sweeteners-deserve-scrutiny/))
— **moderate (quoted wording verified across three independent law-firm summaries; the White House PDF itself
returned 404 to my fetcher)**

**5. FDA built the review machinery and pointed it elsewhere.** Timeline: discussion paper on an enhanced
post-market process, **August 2024**; proposed prioritisation tool published for comment, **18 June 2025**;
request for information on BHA, **February 2026**; programme **finalised** and the first two reassessments —
**BHT and azodicarbonamide** — launched **~12 May 2026**, comments closing **13 July 2026**. Aspartame is not
among the ~32 substances listed under review. Then-commissioner Marty Makary framed it as "Americans want the
FDA to take a fresh look at some of the chemical additives that have become widespread."
([FoodNavigator, 12 May 2026](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/05/12/fda-kicks-off-post-market-food-chemical-review-with-bht-ada/))
— **moderate-to-strong for the events; the FDA's own pages (press release, "Select Chemicals Under FDA Review"
list) returned HTTP 404 to my fetcher and could not be read directly**

**6. FDA's substantive position on aspartame has not changed since 13 July 2023.** It remains an approved food
additive under 21 CFR 172.804 with an ADI of 50 mg/kg bw/day, with no pending restriction. No new FDA statement
on aspartame between 2024 and August 2026 could be found. — **moderate (absence of evidence; FDA pages
unfetchable)**

**7. The live regulatory question is European, and it is overdue.** In a letter to foodwatch dated **20 March
2025** (Ref. Ares(2025)2260700), the Director-General of DG SANTE wrote that as part of the E 962
re-evaluation "EFSA will carefully consider all relevant new studies on aspartame that have become available
since the last risk assessment for aspartame conducted in 2013," explicitly including **IARC Monographs Volume
134 (published September 2024)** and the **96th JECFA meeting (June 2024)**, and that "EFSA will consider
whether a revision of the ADI of aspartame (E 951) is required. The potential update of the dietary exposure
assessment of aspartame (E 951) is part of this work." Completion was "expected… by the end of 2025."
**As of 5 August 2026 EFSA's sweeteners topic page still lists E 962 as "re-evaluation ongoing."**
([Commission letter, PDF hosted by foodwatch](https://www.foodwatch.org/fileadmin/-INT/additives/2025.03.EC_Reply_to_Ms_Sumner_Foodwatch.pdf);
[EFSA sweeteners topic page](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/sweeteners)) — **strong**

**8. EFSA's sweetener programme is active, and it is not moving in one direction.** Acesulfame K re-evaluated
**30 April 2025**: ADI **raised** from the SCF's 9 mg/kg bw/day to **15 mg/kg bw/day**, based on a rat NOAEL of
1,500 mg/kg bw/day, with cancer, glucose/insulin homeostasis and cardiovascular endpoints all judged
unsupported. Neotame, **4 July 2025**. Sucralose, **16 February 2026**: ADI **kept** at 15 mg/kg bw/day, but
EFSA declined to endorse a proposed extension to fine bakery wares because of unresolved uncertainty about
chlorine transfer to organic molecules under prolonged high heat.
([EFSA, acesulfame K, EFSA Journal 2025;23:e9317](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2025.9317);
[EFSA sucralose plain-language summary, 16 Feb 2026](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/plain-language-summary/re-evaluation-sucralose-e-955-food-additive))
— **strong**

**9. The European ban campaign is advocacy, and it failed.** On **4 February 2025** foodwatch, the French
League Against Cancer and Yuka launched a petition asking the Commission and member states to prohibit
aspartame. *(All three are campaigning organisations — treat as advocacy.)* The Commission's reply declined to
act, restated the 40 mg/kg ADI as "protective for the general population," said exposure is "well below" it,
and deferred to the pending EFSA work. — **strong (the reply itself is quoted above)**

**10. Investor pressure exists but has produced no reformulation.** ICCR-member investors filed shareholder
proposals at both Coca-Cola and PepsiCo (**filed 18 April 2024**, annual meetings **1 May 2024**) asking for
third-party reports on health harms from non-sugar sweeteners. *(ICCR is an advocacy coalition.)* Their filing
repeats the contested claim that FDA "reversed its decision in 1981 due to industry pressure" — see
`rumsfeld-conspiracy.md` and `origins.html`; that causal claim is not documented.
([ICCR, 18 Apr 2024](https://www.iccr.org/coca-cola-and-pepsi-to-face-shareholder-proposals-citing-the-potential-health-harms-from-continued-use-of-non-sugar-sweeteners-in-diet-drinks/))
— **moderate; vote outcomes not verified**

**11. No verified aspartame reformulation by a major beverage company in 2024–26.** Diet Coke's aspartame
recipe is unchanged. The frequently-recycled Diet Pepsi story is **2015–16**, not current: PepsiCo replaced
aspartame with sucralose in August 2015, Diet Pepsi volume fell **10.6%** in the following first quarter
(Beverage Digest) versus **5.7%** for aspartame-retaining Diet Coke, and PepsiCo restored an aspartame version
in 2016. Anyone citing it as evidence of a current retreat from aspartame has the decade wrong.
([Food Dive, 2016](https://www.fooddive.com/news/diet-pepsi-is-bringing-aspartame-back-again/517618/)) —
**moderate (trade press, historical)**

**12. New 2025–26 studies not covered elsewhere in this directory.**

- **Wu et al., *Cell Metabolism* 2025;37(5):1075–1088.e7** (epub 19 Feb 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.cmet.2025.01.006).
  0.15% aspartame raised insulin secretion in mice *and monkeys*; subdiaphragmatic vagotomy abolished it;
  aspartame-fed ApoE⁻/⁻ mice grew larger atherosclerotic plaques by an insulin-dependent route; CX3CL1 was the
  top upregulated endothelial gene and deleting *Cx3cr1* in macrophages abolished the effect. Karolinska's own
  release describes the dose as "just over twice the amount that human beverages are allowed to contain," notes
  the mice were genetically prone to atherosclerosis, and states "it is unknown whether aspartame affects
  insulin release in the same way in humans as previous research has produced conflicting results."
  ([PubMed 39978336](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39978336/);
  [Karolinska Institutet, 19 Feb 2025](https://news.ki.se/how-aspartame-affects-blood-vessels-in-mice)) —
  **strong as mechanism, weak as human inference**
- **Aiestaran-Zelaia et al., *Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy*** (epub 20 Dec 2025; Jan 2026 issue),
  "Aspartame decreases fat deposits in mice at a cost of mild cardiac hypertrophy and reduced cognitive
  performance." Male C57BL/6 mice, **one year**, dose stated as **one-sixth of the human ADI**: ~20% reduction
  in fat deposits, mild cardiac hypertrophy on MRI and histology, reduced cardiac output, and Barnes-maze
  deficits. This is the most uncomfortable new paper for a safety site, because the dose is *below* the
  regulatory limit rather than above it.
  ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0753332225010856)) — **moderate as an
  animal finding; single laboratory, not replicated, and the human relevance of one-year mouse endpoints is
  the same open question it always is**
- **Khattab, *Current Nutrition Reports* 2026;15(1):47** (16 May 2026) — narrative review of sweeteners and
  gut microbiota. Explicitly finds responses "highly individualized and dose-dependent"; does **not**
  single out aspartame. ([PubMed 42142239](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42142239/)) — **weak**

## Genuine caveats / what cuts the other way

- **"No regulator has acted" is not the same as "regulators have looked recently."** EFSA's aspartame position
  is still the 2013 opinion. The update was promised for end-2025 and has slipped by more than seven months.
  If it lands and revises the ADI downward, most of this brief ages badly overnight.
- **A US state has, in fact, restricted aspartame.** Louisiana bans it from school meals from 2028–29 and
  requires disclosure from 2028. It is fair to note this was a 40-plus-ingredient omnibus rather than an
  aspartame-specific finding — and unfair to say no jurisdiction has restricted it.
- **The Aiestaran-Zelaia dose is the awkward one.** Most animal papers this site can fairly discount for
  using absurd doses. That one reports effects at ~1/6 of the ADI. The honest response is that it is one
  one-year mouse study awaiting replication, not that the dose was unrealistic.
- **The Cell Metabolism monkey data are a step up from rodent-only work.** The insulin spike was reproduced in
  a primate. The atherosclerosis was not.
- **Political attention is real and growing even where regulation is not.** 140-plus additive bills across 38
  states in 2025; sweeteners are named in a federal health report and in one state statute. Predicting that
  aspartame will never be touched in the US would be an overclaim.

## Myths in this area and what is actually true

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| "RFK Jr./the FDA is reviewing aspartame." | **Not established.** Aspartame is named in the May 2025 MAHA assessment but is not among the substances in FDA's post-market assessment programme, which opened in May 2026 with BHT and azodicarbonamide. |
| "Texas put a cancer warning on diet soda." | **False.** Texas SB 25's 44-ingredient warning list contains no sweeteners. Louisiana's law is the one that names aspartame, and it mandates a neutral QR-code disclosure, not a warning. |
| "Europe is about to ban aspartame." | **False.** The Commission declined the 2025 foodwatch/Yuka/Ligue petition. What is actually pending is an EFSA re-evaluation that *may* revise the ADI. |
| "Pepsi/Coke are quietly removing aspartame." | **Not in this period.** The Diet Pepsi switch was 2015; it was reversed in 2016 after sales fell. |
| "EFSA is tightening the screws on sweeteners." | **Mixed.** It *raised* the acesulfame K ADI from 9 to 15 mg/kg in 2025 and left sucralose's at 15 in 2026, while flagging a high-heat data gap for sucralose. |

## Corrections to the existing site

1. **`caveats.html` (~line 278–281) and `the-evidence.html` (~line 287–290) both say "we found no announced
   timetable for a fresh full aspartame re-evaluation." That is now too strong and should be updated.** The
   European Commission stated in writing on 20 March 2025 that the E 962 re-evaluation will consider all new
   aspartame studies since 2013 — including IARC Vol. 134 and the 96th JECFA — and "will consider whether a
   revision of the ADI of aspartame (E 951) is required," with completion expected by end-2025. Suggested
   replacement: *"EFSA has not announced a standalone re-evaluation of E 951, but the Commission confirmed in
   March 2025 that the pending E 962 opinion will update the aspartame assessment, incorporate IARC and JECFA
   2024, and consider whether the ADI needs revising. It was due by the end of 2025 and, as of August 2026, is
   still listed as ongoing."*
2. **Related, softer: `caveats.html` states "EFSA appears never to have formally responded to IARC's 2023
   classification."** Still literally true — there is no EFSA statement on IARC — but the site should note that
   the Commission has said EFSA will address IARC Vol. 134 inside the E 962 opinion. As written, a reader could
   infer EFSA is ignoring IARC; it has committed not to.
3. **No page currently mentions that a US state has restricted aspartame.** If any page implies aspartame is
   unrestricted everywhere, Louisiana Act 463 needs to appear. I did not find such a sentence, but the caveats
   page should carry Louisiana on its own merits.
